## John Sweeney: a failed attempt to improve an incumbent by adding pullback trades
Source: Technical Analysis of Stocks & Commodities publisher, Shifting To Another Dimension, March1992, https://store.traders.com/-v10-c03-shiftin-pdf.html . PUBLIC_PUBLISHER_EXCERPT_ONLY, displayed paragraphs under Product Description, not the paid PDF/book or figures.
The author describes adding entries when a trending price retraces to a simple average. Some periods looked very profitable, but the adverse-excursion distributions did not distinguish winning from losing add-on trades; he rejected that change. He then discusses elapsed time within a trade as a separate dimension rather than forcing the original entry system to capture every missed trend.
Use for ZEL: retain the positive incumbent; do not infer that every missed profitable trade should be recovered or that a static MAE cut can separate winners from losers. Current hypothesis already records first observable failure time, subsequent next-open exit, original exit and recovered-winner damage across all126. Elapsed time is a diagnostic dimension, not authorization to optimize a new stop timer. The excerpt does not supply a crypto30m threshold or audited external performance.

## Kevin Davey: actual code and both successful-looking and failed protection tests
Source: author site, Algorithmic Trading Tip – Building Risk Protection Into Your Trading, https://kjtradingsystems.com/risk-protection-techniques.html . Read the plain-English rules, EasyLanguage code and written result interpretations for baseline, delay-after-loss, daily/monthly limits, consecutive-loss switch and equity-curve timing. The page's underlying example is a120-minute crude-oil pullback study, explicitly not presented as a recommended strategy. Numerical graph data were not independently extracted/recomputed.
The delay-after-loss modification worsens the author's example; the consecutive-loss shutdown reduces drawdown without improving profits. The author also shows why an equity filter that includes the trade being decided is future leakage, and warns that favorable optimized limits may not transfer out of sample. These are author-reported examples, not independent ZEL results.
Use for ZEL: do not treat 'add a cooldown' as an automatic cure. Timing must use only already-realized outcomes; it cannot prevent earlier positions that are already open. Net, drawdown and skipped recovery trades must be reported separately. None of the author's numeric loss-count, ATR, dollar-limit or moving-average settings are copied into ZEL SSOT. No cooldown/equity filter is implemented by this note.

## Adam H Grimes: why a broken level is not always a broken trade
Source: author article Pattern Development in the GBP, May9,2014, https://www.adamhgrimes.com/pattern-development-in-the-gbp/ . Main text read. The article distinguishes strong contrary momentum during a pullback from a simple retest of a breakout level; it cautions that mechanically using a single level as an absolute exit boundary can shake out a valid position. No exact universal momentum cutoff is supplied.
Use for the currently frozen comparison: the original reversal candle's adverse end is an explicit TEST marker, not proof of complete setup failure. If losses are reduced but recovery winners are sacrificed more, that evidence refutes the marker, not the incumbent or all loss-control development. Source material must establish a distinct observable structure before a next candidate is defined; do not tune the level until the same used-DEV turns green.
