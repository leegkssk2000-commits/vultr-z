## What is new and what remains unknown

The requested causal distinction is between **an adverse condition known while a position still exists**, **an already-scheduled exit**, and **a terminal label only available after the fact**. Saved `reason`, final `mfe_R`/`mae_R`, final exit price and Net cannot establish that distinction. They do not reveal the first qualifying callback, the effective stop before that bar, or whether a supposed improvement was already blocked by the native stop/next-open ordering.

All46 base-cost losses have saved `mfe_R < 1` (verified on the frozen117). This describes conservative observed excursion, not proof that no stop-hit terminal bar ever touched1R: the native engine exits before incorporating that bar's extrema. Its callback-time favorable/adverse ordering is unknown and must be marked unknown, not reconstructed from a favorable high/low. This also separates the current loss46 question from D's protection-after-progress question without claiming that changing protection could never affect a new full chronology.

The single missing artifact is the **ALL117 ordered native held-position trace**, joined to actual entry/touch provenance, with each completed bar's availability time, effective stop before execution, callback input/return, monotone stop after callback, partial/remaining state, pending-exit scheduling and terminal ordering. The stored `native_traces` and `objective_expiry_events` concern admission/setup lifetime; they are not this exit trace. All71 base-cost winners must accompany the46 losses. Final winners, losses, large winners, cost-fragile wins and prior loss-run membership are reporting labels, never policy inputs.

That trace is necessary to determine whether a source-described failure exists **before** the current exit and whether eventual recovery winners share it. It is not sufficient by itself to prove economic benefit: any eventual C-only change requires A's complete chronological comparison including changed occupancy, new/missed entries, unresolved cases and costs.

## Execution contract the observer must preserve

The baseline call chain is `objective_lifetime.build(structural_failure.ExitComparison(True).build(), enabled=True)` around the frozen `_fresh_touch_successor`. Inside its execution adapter, `ExitComparison.single` retains the continuation callback and latest structural exit. Their native `_single` is the authority for order and fills.

1. Entry uses the actual next open only when the completed signal is available and source/segment continuity permits it. Admission can adjust the initial stop through the existing fallback; initial risk is frozen from the admitted stop, not retrospectively from the final stop.
2. At a following bar, an exit already `pending` from the previous completed callback executes at that open before the new bar's intrabar stop test. Without pending exit, the effective stop/gap test precedes target, MFE/MAE update and callback. This is the precise native meaning of stop-first; do not reverse the already-due next-open order.
3. A stop-hit terminal bar does not contribute a high/low to a callback that never occurred. Surviving complete bars update excursions and invoke the existing callback with the available prefix. Late availability/gaps cannot create historical decisions.
4. The callback returns proposals. The engine validates/applies partial, then applies the monotone stop update, then schedules a causal next-open exit or timeout. A proposed next stop is not retrospectively effective during the bar that produced it. `remaining` and `realized_parts_bps` matter for terminal gross; maximum favorable excursion is not a fill.
5. K117 objective expiry, original admission and re-arm remain unchanged. No short-path EMA/ADX reseeding, no outcome/symbol/date key as a feature, and no hidden replacement policy are introduced by observation.

Synthetic falsification must cover mirrored long/short, recovery after adverse prefix, broad-risk winner, large runner, partial remainder, existing pending versus stop, gap/stop-first, same-time decision ordering, source gap/end unresolved, future-bar rejection, disabled identity and duplicated signals/native state. These are requirements linked to executable behavior; passing them cannot be reported as reduced real losses.

## Why no candidate is frozen now

- A terminal `STOP_FIRST` or scratch reason identifies how the engine ended a trade, not when an earlier new causal thesis failure became knowable. The two cost-only base losses must not be called price failures.
- Generic adverse momentum, no progress, old reference, or large risk does not define a new non-overlapping trigger. Adding magnitude, elapsed bars, relative strength thresholds, or outcome-trained boundaries would invent parameters; signal-bar/whole-pullback exits and prior-ADX/handoff/fresh-touch variants are already closed paths.
- A final MFE value can belong to a cost-fragile winner, recovery winner, or eventual loss. Replacing D's profit-protection levels would both overlap ownership and fail C's distinct-axis requirement.
- The appropriate next A action is one exact-incumbent passive run with complete117 parity and availability/priority trace, not a new C economics comparison or another execution of the original K126 observer. The resulting evidence may justify a distinct later rule; absence of one now is not an improvement-impossible claim.

C changes no fee-BE activation, lock height, partial fraction, runner rule, initial stop, cost assumption, lifetime decision or current policy. There is therefore no C policy effect to combine with D and no new measured profit/loss effect. A remains the only economic owner; saved-row review and synthetic observation are separate from A's actual evaluation receipt.
